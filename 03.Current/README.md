# The Conical Cat-and-Mouse Engine

*A nested-cone variant of the coaxial rotary engine — concept paper, v6.0, August 2026.*

## Status

This is a **concept paper about a mechanism**. Nothing here has been built, tested, patented, funded, or reviewed — no prototype, no team, no simulation, no customer, no commercial arrangement. Every claim is a proposal; the paper states no performance numbers because none have been measured.

## The idea

The cat-and-mouse (or scissor) engine is a known rotary type: two coaxial vanes chase each other around a shared chamber, alternately compressing and releasing the gas between them — four power strokes per revolution, no reciprocating parts. This paper proposes one narrow variation: **replace the flat vanes with nested cones.** A cone settling into a cone is self-centring, and the combustion volume at ignition is compact rather than the long thin sliver a Wankel forms. The self-centring advantage is *asserted, not demonstrated*.

## Honest about prior art

The cat-and-mouse engine is a **crowded, long-worked field** — one of the four recognised rotary categories, with real patents ([Galin WO2016201490](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2016201490A1/en), US8511276B2) and a published performance study. This paper does **not** claim "a novel engine with no prior art" (an earlier draft did, falsely). The only claim is the specific nested-cone interface, which the patent searches behind this paper did not surface.

## The one problem that decides everything

**Sealing.** Both cone faces move, at different speeds during compression, past each other at ~60 m/s, above 1500 °C. Sealing an expanding-gas boundary under those conditions is the single hardest thing about the concept, and the reason the cat-and-mouse engine has never displaced the piston. Two unproven routes: a gas-film seal (a maintained thin gas layer, like a gas bearing), or an "open chamber" that abandons physical sealing and accepts leakage. The concept has **no honest future until one is shown to work.**

## The experiment that would settle it

Not an engine — a bench rig: a pair of representative cones driven at operating speed and temperature, no combustion, measuring directly whether a gas film can hold a representative pressure without contact. Clean pass/fail. If it can't, the sealed variant is dead; if the open-chamber leakage is too large, the whole concept is dead — and that negative result is worth publishing so no one else spends years on it.

## About earlier versions

Earlier drafts were written as an "investment ready" funded programme, naming a hired deputy investigator, legal opinions from named law firms, an independent due-diligence engagement, customer letters of intent, secured insurance, and a built-and-tested demonstrator. **None of that existed** — no person hired, no firm engaged, no letter obtained, nothing built. All removed. No one has been contacted regarding this work. (These drafts were never published; the removal is for the record.)
